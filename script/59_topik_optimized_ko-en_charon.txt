Hello, and welcome to today's comprehensive TOPIK Korean lesson. I'm so glad you're here. Whether you are completely new to Korean or just brushing up on the basics, this lesson is designed for you. In the next ten minutes, we will cover four essential topics together. First, you will master the most common Korean greetings. Second, you will learn how to introduce yourself in Korean. Third, we will study the foundational grammar patterns that every beginner must know. And finally, we will practice five practical expressions that you can use right away in real life. Are you ready? Let's start with greetings. The most important Korean greeting is 안녕하세요. Listen slowly one more time. 안녕하세요. And now at a natural pace. 안녕하세요. This phrase literally means "are you at peace," but in everyday conversation, it simply means "hello." You can use it any time of day, morning, afternoon, or evening, with strangers, with elders, or in any polite situation. Now, when it is time to part ways, Korean has two different goodbyes, and choosing the right one matters. If you are leaving and the other person is staying behind, you say 안녕히 계세요. Slowly. 안녕히 계세요. Naturally. 안녕히 계세요. The word 계세요 means "please stay," so this phrase means "stay in peace." But if the other person is leaving and you are the one staying, you say 안녕히 가세요. Slowly. 안녕히 가세요. Naturally. 안녕히 가세요. Here, 가세요 means "please go," so the phrase means "go in peace." Next, when you meet someone for the first time, you say 만나서 반갑습니다. Slowly. 만나서 반갑습니다. Naturally. 만나서 반갑습니다. This means "I am happy to meet you." And finally, a friendly phrase to wish someone well, 잘 지내세요. Slowly. 잘 지내세요. Naturally. 잘 지내세요. This means "take care" or "be well."

Now let's move on to self-introduction. To say "I am" plus your name in formal Korean, the pattern is 저는, then your name, then 입니다. 저는 means "as for me," and 입니다 is the formal copula meaning "am" or "is." For example. 저는 마이클입니다. Slowly. 저는 마이클입니다. Naturally. 저는 마이클입니다. This means "I am Michael." A slight variation is 제 이름은, then your name, then 입니다, which means "my name is." 제 이름은 마이클입니다. To say where you are from, use the pattern 저는, country name, plus 에서 왔어요. The particle 에서 means "from," and 왔어요 is the past tense of "to come." For example. 저는 미국에서 왔어요. Slowly. 저는 미국에서 왔어요. Naturally. 저는 미국에서 왔어요. This means "I came from America." To say what you are studying, use 한국어를 공부하고 있습니다, which means "I am studying Korean." Slowly. 한국어를 공부하고 있습니다. Naturally. 한국어를 공부하고 있습니다. The pattern -고 있습니다 indicates a continuing action. To talk about your job, you can say 저는, occupation, plus 입니다. For example, 저는 학생입니다 means "I am a student." 저는 회사원입니다 means "I am an office worker." And to mention a hobby, the pattern is 제 취미는, hobby, plus 입니다. For example. 제 취미는 독서입니다. This means "my hobby is reading."

Now let's study some foundational grammar. Korean has two main politeness levels you need to know as a beginner. The formal level uses -ㅂ니다 or -입니다, and you hear it in news broadcasts, business meetings, and respectful situations. The polite informal level uses -이에요 or -예요, and it is used in everyday friendly conversation. Both are polite, but the formal level is more respectful. For example, "I am a student" is 저는 학생입니다 in the formal level, and 저는 학생이에요 in the polite informal level. Both are correct, but choose based on the situation. To make a question in the formal style, change -입니다 to -입니까. For example. 학생입니까? This means "are you a student?" The intonation rises slightly at the end, just as in English questions. Slowly. 학생입니까? Naturally. 학생입니까? Next, let's learn negation. There are two ways to make a negative sentence. The simple way is to put 안 in front of the verb. For example, 가다 means "to go," so 안 가요 means "I do not go." Slowly. 안 가요. The longer form is to add -지 않다 to the verb stem. So 가다 becomes 가지 않아요. Both mean the same thing. Finally, the most important particles you must know are 은 and 는, which mark the topic, and 이 and 가, which mark the subject. Use 은 after a word ending in a consonant, and 는 after a vowel. So 저는, ending in vowel, takes 는, while 책은, "the book," ending in consonant, takes 은. Use 이 after a consonant and 가 after a vowel for subjects. The topic markers introduce what the sentence is about, while the subject markers identify who or what is doing the action. This distinction takes time to master, so do not worry if it feels confusing at first.

Now for our practical expressions. These five phrases will help you in everyday situations. First, to express thanks politely, say 감사합니다. Slowly. 감사합니다. Naturally. 감사합니다. This means "thank you." A more casual version is 고맙습니다. Both are widely used. Second, to apologize, say 죄송합니다. Slowly. 죄송합니다. Naturally. 죄송합니다. This means "I am sorry," and it works for almost any apology, big or small. Third, to ask for help, say 도와주세요. Slowly. 도와주세요. Naturally. 도와주세요. This means "please help me," and it can be a real lifesaver when you are lost or confused. Fourth, when shopping or asking about prices, say 얼마예요? Slowly. 얼마예요? Naturally. 얼마예요? This means "how much is it?" Point at the item and ask. Very useful at markets and shops. Fifth and finally, when you do not catch what someone said, you can politely ask them to repeat by saying 한 번 더 말씀해 주세요. Slowly. 한 번 더 말씀해 주세요. Naturally. 한 번 더 말씀해 주세요. This means "please say it one more time." It is much more polite than just saying "what."
